We all hope our lives will amount to something.

But what makes a life meaningful? Do we have to accomplish something great for our years on earth to count?

Today I want to share a very small story.

Just a little home, a pot of tea, and a great many ordinary afternoons.

This story comes to us from the evangelist Sam Kamaleson, and preachers have been retelling it ever since.

---

## A New Believer at Seventy

In Melbourne, Australia, there lived an elderly woman who came to faith in Christ at the age of seventy.

At an age when most people are looking back on their lives, her life of faith was only beginning. Like many new believers, she had one simple longing: to serve the Lord who had saved her. The trouble was, she had no idea what she could do.

So she went to see her pastor.

"I believe God is calling me to some kind of ministry," she told him. "But what should I do?"

Her pastor had no idea what to suggest. "Well," he said at last, "maybe you should go home and pray about it."

That is classic pastoral advice when you don't have a better answer.

But she took him at his word.

## What She Already Had

So she went home and prayed.

And as she prayed, she began to take stock of what God had already placed in her hands.

People had often told her she had a gift for hospitality. She lived alone, and her afternoons were free. Her home was only a few blocks from the University of Melbourne.

And the university was full of international students: young people far from their families and from everything familiar, studying in a foreign country, speaking a language not their own, and going home each evening to rooms where no one was waiting for them.

Then an idea came to her, so simple it seemed almost foolish.

## A Packet of Cards

She went down to the pharmacy and bought a packet of index cards. On every card she wrote:

**"Are you homesick? Come to my house at 4:00 p.m. for tea."**

Underneath she added her address and phone number.

Then this seventy-year-old woman set off across the University of Melbourne campus with her stack of cards, leaving them wherever students might see them: on bulletin boards, in dormitory hallways, in dining halls, on restroom mirrors, even on the windshields of parked cars.

When the last card was gone, she went home, put the kettle on, and set out the tea.

## Fourteen Afternoons

At four o'clock on the first afternoon, no one came.

No one came on the second day, either. Or the third.

The fifth day came and went. Then the seventh. The tenth. The eleventh, the twelfth, the thirteenth. The fourteenth.

Every afternoon at four, the tea was ready.

Every afternoon at four, no one knocked.

But she did not give up, and she did not withdraw her invitation. She kept praying, and she kept making tea, just in case.

When we tell this story, it is easy to remember the ending and hurry past those fourteen days. Yet this is very often what faithfulness looks like: no response, no visible fruit, just a kettle coming to the boil one more time.

## The Fifteenth Day

At four o'clock on the fifteenth afternoon, there was a knock at the door.

Standing on her doorstep was a young student from Indonesia.

He was homesick and longing for someone to talk to. She invited him in, poured him a cup of tea, and listened as he told her his story.

He was eager to talk.

She was just as eager to listen.

When he got back to campus, he told his friends, "Hey, you won't believe it! I met a lady that's just like my grandmother."

## More Than a Cup of Tea

From then on, the knocks came more often.

One student brought another. At first there were only a few; then more and more began to come. Many came because they missed home. But in time it became clear that what they longed for was more than a cup of tea.

She poured their tea, listened to their stories, and told them the good news of Jesus Christ.

Those afternoons went on for ten years.

Over that decade, hundreds of international students passed through her door. Many of them, in that little living room, came to know Jesus Christ: his forgiveness, and the new life he gives.

## Seventy Pallbearers

She died in her early eighties.

At her funeral there were seventy honorary pallbearers.[^1]

They had come from Indonesia, Malaysia, India, Pakistan, and many other countries. Each of them had once sat in her living room at four o'clock with a cup of tea. And there, each of them had met Christ.

Gathered from the far corners of the earth, these seventy walked with her on her final journey.

Each of them had once found a home in hers.

## Reflection

**Her calling did not begin with a vision.**

It began with an ordinary question and an answer that sounded almost like a shrug. God often leads us this way. He rarely shows us the whole road at once; he simply asks us to take the next step.

After she prayed, she was given no grand plan, only a packet of cards, a pot of tea, and fourteen afternoons of waiting for someone who did not come.

**She served with what she already had.**

A home near a university. A few free hours each afternoon. A willingness to listen.

She did not wait to become someone else before she began to serve. She simply placed what she already had into the Lord's hands.

**In Scripture, hospitality is far more than good manners.**

Jesus said, "I was a stranger, and you took me in" (Matthew 25:35).

The gospel is also a story of divine hospitality. Through Christ, God welcomes those who were once strangers into his household. As Paul writes:

"So then you are no longer strangers and foreigners, but you are fellow citizens with the saints, and of the household of God" (Ephesians 2:19).

The warmth those homesick students found in her living room was a foretaste of a far greater home.

**She was following in her Lord's footsteps.**

Jesus said, "For the Son of Man also came not to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45).

At seventy, she boiled water, poured tea, and listened to strangers. In those small, hidden acts of service, she was walking the road her Lord had walked before her.

So we might ask ourselves:

What has God already placed in my hands?

And who, living not far from me, is longing for home?

## Prayer

Lord, what I hold in my hands seems so little.

Open my eyes to the people you have already placed around me.

Help me see what you have already entrusted to me, even if it is only a room, an afternoon, and a pot of tea.

On the days when no one comes, keep me faithful: still making the tea, still keeping the door open.

May every stranger I welcome catch a glimpse of the home you are preparing for us.

In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord.

Amen.
