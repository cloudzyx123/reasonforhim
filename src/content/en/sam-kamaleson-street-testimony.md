**Sam Kamaleson’s Testimony of Faith and Calling**

*Edited by Richard Zhang*

On a crowded street in Madras, India—now Chennai—a young man stood beside a dustbin, closed his eyes, and began to sing.

He was no street performer, and he had no wish to draw attention to himself. The very thing he feared was being taken for a fool. But when he opened his eyes, something happened that he had not expected.

The young man was Sam Kamaleson. In the years that followed, he would tell the story of that day again and again.

The story begins earlier. In the 1950s, Kamaleson was studying veterinary medicine in Madras. His family had been Christian for generations. Yet he noticed that his roommate, a former Hindu, had become a different man. When Kamaleson asked what had happened, his roommate said he had become a Christian; the only explanation he could give was that he had met Jesus. “How can you meet Jesus?” Kamaleson later recalled thinking. It was then that he realized that, for all his Christian upbringing, he was not really a Christian himself. His roommate, as it turned out, had come to faith through a team of street preachers.

What follows is Kamaleson’s account, edited for reading from an excerpt of one of his recorded talks. The recording begins partway through his story.

## 1. You Must Be Born Again

“You must be born again.”[^1] The rationale was: if it was true for Nicodemus, it certainly is true for me. We knelt underneath a tree and began to pray. When I got up off my knees, I had invited Christ into my heart.

That day a walk began that has not ended yet, and it changed me. My ambitions and desires were changed.

## 2. A Conversation in the Street

I was walking down a crowded part of Purasawalkam—now, that’s probably a tongue twister, but it’s a part of Chennai, a rather crowded part of Chennai. As I walked down its streets, Jesus talked to me. He said, “Hey, don’t you think these people around you need to know me?”

I said, “Of course they should.”

Then He said, “You tell them. Somebody has to tell them. You tell them.”

I said, “I can’t do that. They are going in twenty different directions. How can I stop them?”

He said, “Sing. They will stop.”

“Here in the street?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“They’ll think I’m a fool.”

Now here’s the answer He gave me: “You’re already a fool.”[^2]

Let me give you the reason He gave me. If you tell anybody that you are now indwelt by Jesus Christ, the first thing they think is, *What a fool!* They may not tell you that—it depends how well bred they are. But *what a fool! What a stupid fool!* That’s what He was referring to.

Then He said to me, “You don’t have to be known as a fool because you sing. You are known as a fool because you claim submission to me.”

## 3. Singing Beside a Dustbin

I had no way to wiggle out of Him. So I found a dustbin. Not too many self-respecting people are found around dustbins. I stood there and closed my eyes. It was desperately destructive to do what I was asked to do. Then I sang.

When I opened my eyes, there was a crowd standing there. In India, if two dogs fight, a crowd will gather, so it’s not difficult to gather a crowd. Now I had to do something.

I said three things:

1. He—Yesu—is alive.[^3]
2. I have found that He is alive.
3. You also can find that He is alive.

Everything inside of me dried up. I had said what I needed to say. Now I had to escape this crowd.

## 4. “Tell Me”

I tried to push my way out. A man caught hold of my hand and said, “I want to know. Tell me.” I was totally surprised.

So I sat down at the edge of the sidewalk. I had a hip-pocket New Testament; I pulled it out and showed him verse after verse. The guy ate it up.[^4] I couldn’t feed him enough.

I took his address and ran back to the guys I had led to the Lord, who were living with me at the college. I gathered them and said, “There’s plenty of fish outside. Let’s go fishing.”[^5]

Try some way to throw a line into a crowd. You will be surprised: there are people anxiously waiting to discover whether Christ is alive or not—whether He can do anything for them personally. We haven’t tried. Most of us are used to expressing our faith, our testimony, within a protected environment. We don’t like the discomfort of being exposed. It’s raw. It could get nasty. Why invite trouble like that?

For three and a half years after that, we went into the streets regularly—sometimes three times a week. It was there that He tapped me on the shoulder and said, “I want you. Come, follow me.”

Oh, they told me, “If you come to this part again, we’ll break every bone in your body.” I did go. Not one bone is broken yet.

## 5. Only Through Obedience

God is with you when you obey Him. Something changes in you so radically that only obedience can lead you into that. Fascination, wonder, imagination running wild—all of that is great. But obedience, one step of obedience, will lead you into an enormous, unending pathway of change, of transformation in your life.

I did graduate as a veterinarian, and then went on to theological studies and had the distinction of being a pastor of a church. But all of that is simply to say this: when He says He will make you, He is faithful and true in transforming you. And the result is that you too become a fisher of men.

## About Sam Kamaleson

Samuel Theodore Kamaleson (1930–2021) was born in Vellore, India. When he finished high school, his mother took him to church, prayed, gave thanks, and told him she had dedicated him to the ministry. That, she said, was why she had named him Samuel—the name of the boy Hannah gave to the Lord (1 Samuel 1:27–28). As he recalled it decades later, he protested that she had not consulted him. Then, feeling sorry, he added, “If I become no good for anything, then I’ll become a minister.” His mother replied, “You be careful, because God is listening.”

After graduating from Madras Veterinary College, he went to Asbury Theological Seminary in Kentucky, where he studied sacred music first and then theology.

Returning to India, he twice served as pastor of Emmanuel Methodist Church in Chennai (1961–1968 and 1971–1974). In 1974 he became a vice president of World Vision International and went on to lead pastors’ conferences in dozens of countries until he retired in 1996. He also founded Bethel Agricultural Fellowship in 1963 to house and educate poor and orphaned children; it later grew to include a hospital and a school.

Pastors in his native Tamil Nadu often called him *Annan*—“elder brother.” He died on February 28, 2021, at the age of ninety.

## Editorial Notes

<!-- footnotes-position -->

[^1]: **Born again.** See Jesus’ conversation with Nicodemus in John 3:1–8, especially verse 7.

[^2]: **“You’re already a fool.”** Compare 1 Corinthians 4:10. In a [2010 interview](https://faithandleadership.com/the-real-mentor), Kamaleson told this exchange somewhat differently. As the interviewer summarized it, the voice told him he was already a fool; the question was whether he would be “a silent, stupid fool or a common fool filled with the joy of Christ, able to speak his truth.”

[^3]: **Yesu.** *Yesu* is the Tamil form of the name Jesus. In the 2010 interview, Kamaleson remembered his first words differently: “He is alive!”—adding, “I didn’t even say who the ‘he’ was.”

[^4]: **His first convert.** According to [Kamaleson’s obituary](https://foxandweeks.com/obituaries/o/8099/Rev.-Dr.-Samuel-Theodore-Kamaleson), the man who stopped him to ask about Jesus was the first person he led to the Lord.

[^5]: **Fishing.** The fishing language that runs through this account—“Let’s go fishing,” “throw a line,” “Come, follow me,” “He will make you,” “a fisher of men”—echoes Jesus’ call to His first disciples in Matthew 4:19 (compare Mark 1:17).
